# Starting points by field

Well-established, mostly free sources. They are starting points, not recommendations: always verify the
specific page or item for the student's topic and level before listing it.

## Across fields

- **OpenStax** (openstax.org): free peer-reviewed introductory textbooks (physics, chemistry, biology,
  calculus, statistics, economics, psychology, sociology, history).
- **MIT OpenCourseWare** (ocw.mit.edu): full course materials, lecture videos, problem sets with solutions.
- **LibreTexts** (libretexts.org): open textbooks across STEM and humanities.
- **Khan Academy** (khanacademy.org): short explanations and exercises, school to early university.
- **University library**: e-books and databases the student can already access (ask for the library URL).
- **Google Scholar / Semantic Scholar / CORE / arXiv**: papers; prefer open versions.

## Mathematics, physics, CS, engineering

- 3Blue1Brown (visual intuition: linear algebra, calculus, neural networks), Paul's Online Math Notes,
  Wolfram MathWorld (reference), Art of Problem Solving (problem solving).
- Physics: Feynman Lectures on Physics (free online edition at feynmanlectures.caltech.edu),
  HyperPhysics (concept maps).
- CS: CS50 (Harvard), MIT 6.006 / 6.042 on OCW, VisuAlgo (algorithm animations), official language and
  library documentation, Python Tutor (step-through visualiser).
- Practice: Project Euler (maths + programming), LeetCode/Exercism (programming), past papers from the
  course.

## Life sciences, medicine, chemistry

- NCBI Bookshelf (free textbooks such as *Molecular Biology of the Cell* older editions, StatPearls),
  OpenStax Biology / Anatomy & Physiology / Chemistry.
- Chem LibreTexts; Royal Society of Chemistry education resources.
- Medicine: the course's recommended textbooks and the library's clinical databases; guidelines from
  national bodies (e.g. NICE, AWMF) for clinical questions.

## Philosophy

- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy** (plato.stanford.edu) and **Internet Encyclopedia of
  Philosophy** (iep.utm.edu): authoritative, peer-reviewed entries with bibliographies.
- PhilPapers (philpapers.org) for literature; primary texts via Project Gutenberg or the course reader.
- Podcasts: *History of Philosophy without any gaps* (Peter Adamson), *Philosophy Bites*.

## Literature, history, languages, arts

- Project Gutenberg and Wikisource (public-domain primary texts), the Internet Archive (scans, with
  care about rights), Perseus Digital Library (classics).
- Literary criticism via the library's databases (JSTOR, MLA International Bibliography).
- History: primary-source collections of national libraries and archives; Crash Course for overviews.
- Languages: the course textbook's audio, Forvo (pronunciation), Wiktionary (etymology, inflection).
- Art and music: museum collections online (Rijksmuseum, The Met Open Access), IMSLP (scores).

## Economics, business, law, social science

- CORE Econ (*The Economy*, free), OpenStax Economics, Marginal Revolution University (short videos).
- Data: OECD, World Bank, Eurostat, national statistics offices.
- Law: official legislation portals of the jurisdiction, court databases, the course casebook; never
  rely on summaries for exam answers in law without checking the primary source.
- Social science methods: SAGE Research Methods (via library), OpenIntro Statistics (free).
